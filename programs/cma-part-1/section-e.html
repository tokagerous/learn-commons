Unit 1 · E.1 GRC

Internal control objectives & COSO

Internal control is "a process, effected by an entity's board, management, and other personnel, designed to provide reasonable assurance regarding the achievement of objectives" — the COSO definition. Three categories of objectives, five components, seventeen principles.

§1.1Three categories of objectives

  • Operations — effectiveness and efficiency of operations, safeguarding of assets.
  • Reporting — reliability of internal and external (financial and non-financial) reporting.
  • Compliance — adherence to laws and regulations.

§1.2The five COSO components (CRIME)

A common mnemonic is CRIME: Control environment, Risk assessment, Information & communication, Monitoring, control activitiEs.

ComponentWhat it covers
Control environmentTone at the top, ethics, board oversight, structure
Risk assessmentIdentify and analyze risks to objectives
Control activitiesPolicies and procedures (segregation, authorization, reconciliation, IT controls)
Information & communicationCapture, processing, and reporting of relevant information
MonitoringOngoing and periodic evaluations of the control system
Limitation

Even well-designed controls only provide REASONABLE — never absolute — assurance. Inherent limits: human error, collusion, management override, cost-benefit constraints.

Practice 1 — COSO categories

Which is NOT one of COSO's three categories of objectives?

  • Operations
  • Reporting
  • Profitability
  • Compliance
Why: Profitability is an operational outcome, not a control-objective category. The three categories are Operations, Reporting, and Compliance — sometimes abbreviated ORC. Don't confuse them with the five COSO components (CRIME).

Practice 2 — Inherent limitations

Which is an INHERENT limitation of internal control?

  • Management override of controls
  • Insufficient training of new staff
  • Outdated accounting software
  • Lack of monitoring procedures
Why: Inherent limitations exist regardless of design quality: human error, collusion, management override, cost-benefit. Insufficient training, outdated software, and missing monitoring are design or operating deficiencies — fixable.

Practice 3 — Tone at the top

"Tone at the top" most closely relates to which COSO component?

  • Risk assessment
  • Control activities
  • Monitoring
  • Control environment
Why: Control environment encompasses board oversight, ethics, integrity, and management's commitment to control — the foundation everything else rests on. Without strong tone at the top, no amount of control activities prevents fraud.
Unit 2 · E.1 GRC

Control activities & segregation of duties

Control activities are the day-to-day mechanisms — authorizations, reconciliations, reviews, IT controls. The single most important is segregation of duties (SoD): split incompatible functions among different people so no one person can both perpetrate and conceal a fraud.

§2.1The ARC framework

Segregate three duties: Authorization (approving the transaction), Recording (the bookkeeping), and Custody (physical control of the asset). One person doing two of three creates fraud opportunity.

Risk if combinedMitigation
Custody + RecordingSteal asset, hide via book entry
Authorization + CustodyAuthorize disbursement to self, take cash
Authorization + RecordingApprove fictitious transactions, record at will

§2.2Other key control activities

  • Authorization — general (policies) or specific (transaction-by-transaction).
  • Documentation — pre-numbered, complete, accurate.
  • Reconciliations — bank, subledger to GL, physical count to perpetual.
  • Independent verification — second person reviews.
  • Physical safeguards — locks, vaults, access cards.

Practice 1 — SoD

Which combination violates segregation of duties?

  • Sales clerk takes orders; warehouse ships
  • Cashier receives checks AND posts to AR ledger
  • Storeroom counts inventory; perpetual is maintained by accounting
  • Accounts payable matches invoice to PO
Why: Receiving cash (custody) AND posting AR (recording) lets the cashier steal cash and record a fictitious credit (lapping). Other options keep ARC roles separate.

Practice 2 — Compensating control

A small office has only two accounting employees, making perfect SoD impossible. The BEST compensating control is:

  • Hire more staff (or outsource bookkeeping)
  • Trust both employees
  • Active owner review of bank reconciliations and key journal entries
  • Disable the accounting software's edit logs
Why: When SoD is structurally impossible (small business), strong oversight by an independent party (the owner) compensates. Insist on personal bank-statement review and signature on disbursements above a threshold.

Practice 3 — Authorization type

"All purchases above $10,000 require CFO sign-off" is an example of:

  • Specific authorization
  • General authorization
  • Pre-authorization
  • Custodial authorization
Why: Specific authorization requires individual approval per transaction (above the threshold). General authorization sets policy ranges within which staff act on their own (e.g. "discounts up to 10% are pre-approved").
Unit 3 · E.1 GRC

Risk assessment & deficiency remediation

Identify risks to control objectives, evaluate likelihood × impact, and design controls to address them. When a control fails or doesn't exist, classify the deficiency: control deficiency, significant deficiency, or material weakness.

§3.1Severity ladder

TermDefinition (PCAOB AS 5)Reporting
Control deficiencyDesign or operation does not allow controls to prevent/detect a misstatement on a timely basisInternal only
Significant deficiencyLess severe than material weakness, but important enough to merit attention by those charged with governanceAudit committee
Material weaknessReasonable possibility of material misstatementPublic disclosure (10-K, SOX 404)

§3.2Testing methods

  • Inquiry — ask staff. Cheapest, weakest evidence.
  • Observation — watch the control in action.
  • Inspection — examine evidence (signed forms, system logs).
  • Reperformance — auditor re-runs the control. Strongest, most expensive.

Practice 1 — Severity

A deficiency that creates a reasonable possibility of a material misstatement going undetected is:

  • An immaterial issue
  • A control deficiency
  • A significant deficiency
  • A material weakness
Why: Material weakness = reasonable possibility of material misstatement. Public companies must disclose material weaknesses in their 10-K under SOX 404. Below that severity: significant deficiency (governance only) or control deficiency (internal only).

Practice 2 — Testing strength

Which testing method provides the STRONGEST evidence about control operating effectiveness?

  • Reperformance
  • Observation
  • Inquiry
  • Inspection of documentation
Why: Reperformance — the auditor independently re-executes the control — gives direct evidence the control works as designed. Inquiry is weakest (people lie or misremember); observation captures only the moment observed; inspection requires reliable documentation.

Practice 3 — Risk response

A risk with both low likelihood and low impact is best:

  • Mitigated through detailed control activities
  • Accepted (no control investment beyond monitoring)
  • Transferred via insurance
  • Avoided by exiting the activity
Why: The four risk responses are accept, mitigate, transfer, avoid. Low/low risks rarely justify the cost of dedicated controls — accept and monitor. Reserve mitigation for high-impact or high-likelihood risks.
Unit 4 · E.1 GRC

Corporate governance, SOX, and external audit

After Enron and WorldCom, the Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX, 2002) overhauled public-company governance. Section 302 makes the CEO and CFO certify financial statements. Section 404 requires management's assertion on ICFR (internal control over financial reporting) and an external-auditor attestation.

§4.1Key SOX provisions

  • §302 — CEO/CFO certify accuracy of FS and disclosure controls each quarter.
  • §404(a) — Management's annual ICFR assessment.
  • §404(b) — Auditor attestation on ICFR (only large accelerated filers).
  • §802 — Penalties for document destruction; 7-year audit-records retention.
  • §906 — Criminal certification of FS by CEO/CFO ($5M / 20 years).

SOX created the PCAOB (Public Company Accounting Oversight Board) to oversee public-company auditors, displacing prior self-regulation by the AICPA.

§4.2Audit committee independence

SOX requires audit committees be composed of independent directors. They — not management — hire, fire, and compensate the external auditor. At least one member must be a "financial expert" (or the company must explain why not).

Practice 1 — SOX 404

Which SOX section requires management's annual assessment of internal control over financial reporting?

  • Section 302
  • Section 802
  • Section 404
  • Section 906
Why: §404(a): management's annual ICFR assertion. §404(b): auditor's attestation (large accelerated filers). §302: quarterly CEO/CFO certifications. §906: criminal-liability certification. §802: document retention.

Practice 2 — Audit committee

Under SOX, the external auditor is hired by:

  • The audit committee of the board
  • The CFO
  • The CEO with shareholder ratification
  • The PCAOB
Why: Pre-SOX, management hired the auditor — creating a direct conflict (the auditee paying the auditor's bills). SOX moves auditor engagement to the audit committee, which must be entirely independent directors. PCAOB regulates auditors but doesn't engage them for individual audits.

Practice 3 — PCAOB

The PCAOB is responsible for:

  • Setting U.S. GAAP
  • Setting auditing standards for public-company audits
  • Auditing public companies directly
  • Enforcing SEC reporting requirements
Why: PCAOB sets auditing standards (AS 1, AS 5, etc.), inspects registered firms, and disciplines auditors. GAAP is set by FASB. The SEC enforces reporting; PCAOB enforces audit quality.
Unit 5 · E.2 System Controls

General accounting system controls (ITGC)

IT general controls are the foundation that lets all other application controls work. They cover access, change management, operations, and the broader IT environment. Without sound ITGCs, application controls can be bypassed.

§5.1Four ITGC domains

  • Access controls — who can see/edit what; user provisioning, password policy, role-based access, privileged-account review.
  • Change management — segregation between developers and production; testing; documented approvals; emergency-change protocols.
  • Computer operations — job scheduling, monitoring, incident handling, backup execution.
  • Program development — SDLC discipline; user acceptance testing.

§5.2Access principles

Least privilege: each user gets only the access they need to do their job — nothing more. Separation of duties in IT: developers don't deploy to production; admins don't run business transactions.

Common finding

Excessive access to "super-user" or admin accounts is the #1 ITGC issue auditors find. Quarterly access reviews are the standard remediation.

Practice 1 — ITGC purpose

IT general controls primarily exist to:

  • Replace application controls
  • Test transaction accuracy
  • Provide a reliable foundation under which application controls can operate
  • Encrypt customer data only
Why: ITGCs (access, change, operations) don't test transactions directly — they ensure the environment in which application controls run is trustworthy. If anyone can modify production code, no application control can be relied upon.

Practice 2 — Least privilege

The principle of least privilege requires that users:

  • Have only the access needed for their role
  • Have full access to all systems for flexibility
  • Share credentials within a department
  • Use the same password across applications
Why: Least privilege confines damage from compromise or insider threat. The opposites all violate access discipline. Modern implementation: role-based access control (RBAC) with periodic recertification.

Practice 3 — Change management

Allowing the same person to develop AND deploy production code most directly violates:

  • Backup and recovery
  • Data encryption
  • Physical security
  • Segregation of duties in change management
Why: Developer-to-production segregation prevents unauthorized code (malicious or accidental) from reaching live systems. Auditors expect a separate release/operations role to own deployment.
Unit 6 · E.2 System Controls

Application & transaction controls

Within an application, controls validate that data is complete, accurate, valid, and authorized. Three control points: input, processing, output.

§6.1Input controls

  • Edit checks: range (age 0-120), format (email pattern), validity (vendor on master).
  • Check digits: detect transposition errors in account numbers.
  • Field requirements: mandatory fields prevent incomplete entry.
  • Limit/reasonableness: invoice amount < 3× moving average flagged.

§6.2Processing & output controls

Processing: hash totals, run-to-run controls, sequence checks, audit logs of changes. Output: distribution lists, watermarks, reconciliation totals to source documents.

Cue

Preventive controls stop errors before entry (input edits). Detective controls identify them after the fact (reconciliations). Corrective controls remediate (auto-resubmit, error-queue review).

Practice 1 — Edit check type

A field rejecting "MM/DD/YYYY" input that doesn't match a valid calendar date is a:

  • Sequence check
  • Validity (format) check
  • Limit check
  • Reasonableness check
Why: Validity tests whether the value is structurally valid (a real date, an existing customer ID). Limit checks numeric bounds; reasonableness compares against typical patterns.

Practice 2 — Preventive vs detective

A monthly reconciliation between subsidiary ledger and general ledger is a:

  • Detective control
  • Preventive control
  • Corrective control
  • Steering control
Why: Reconciliations find errors after they occur — that's detection. Preventive examples: input edits, authorization, segregation. Corrective: error-handling routines that fix or resubmit.

Practice 3 — Hash total

A "hash total" verifies:

  • Each transaction is profitable
  • Sales tax is computed correctly
  • The same data was processed end-to-end without loss or change
  • Customer addresses are valid
Why: A hash total is the sum of a non-meaningful field (e.g. all customer IDs) computed at input and re-checked at processing. If they don't match, records were lost or modified. Hash totals don't have business meaning — that's the point.
Unit 7 · E.2 System Controls

Technology, data, and cyber controls

Beyond the accounting system: encryption, network security, identity management, data classification, and incident response. The CMA tests vocabulary; you need to recognize each control's purpose.

§7.1Technical safeguards

  • Encryption at rest (database/disk) and in transit (TLS).
  • Firewalls & intrusion detection/prevention (IDS/IPS).
  • Multi-factor authentication (MFA) — something you know + have/are.
  • VPN / zero-trust network access.
  • Data loss prevention (DLP) tools.

§7.2Data classification

Public, internal, confidential, restricted. Each tier triggers different controls (encryption strength, access reviews, retention).

Practice 1 — MFA

Multi-factor authentication strengthens access control by combining:

  • Two or more independent credential types (know / have / are)
  • Two passwords from the same user
  • Two different applications using the same login
  • Username and email confirmation only
Why: MFA requires factors from different categories — something you KNOW (password), something you HAVE (token, phone), something you ARE (biometric). Two passwords or two prompts of the same kind aren't multi-factor.

Practice 2 — Encryption purpose

Encryption "at rest" specifically protects data when:

  • Being entered by a user
  • Being transmitted across a network
  • Stored in databases or files
  • Being processed in memory
Why: "At rest" = stored. "In transit" = moving across networks. Both are necessary; neither protects data being processed in memory (a separate problem solved by trusted execution environments). A laptop disk encryption is at-rest protection.

Practice 3 — DLP

A data loss prevention (DLP) tool primarily aims to:

  • Encrypt all incoming email
  • Restore lost backup files
  • Replace antivirus software
  • Detect and block unauthorized exfiltration of sensitive data
Why: DLP scans outbound channels (email, USB, web uploads) for content matching sensitive-data patterns (SSN, account numbers) and blocks or alerts on attempted leaks. It's a complement to, not a replacement for, encryption and antivirus.
Unit 8 · E.2 System Controls

Backup, recovery, and business continuity

Backups protect against data loss; disaster recovery (DR) and business continuity planning (BCP) protect against operations loss. The two acronyms you must know cold: RTO (recovery time objective — how long you can be down) and RPO (recovery point objective — how much data you can afford to lose).

§8.1Backup strategy

  • Full: complete copy. Slow, large, easy to restore.
  • Incremental: only what changed since last backup. Fast, small, slow to restore (chain).
  • Differential: everything since last full. Middle ground.
  • 3-2-1 rule: 3 copies, 2 different media, 1 offsite.

§8.2RTO, RPO, and DR site types

SiteStateRTOCost
HotFully running, data replicatedMinutesHighest
WarmHardware ready, partial dataHoursMedium
ColdEmpty space, hardware to installDays–WeeksLowest
Untested = unproven

Backup tapes that have never been restored may be corrupt. DR sites that have never been failed-over may not work. Periodic disaster simulations are part of the control, not optional.

Practice 1 — RPO

A 1-hour RPO means:

  • The system can be down up to 1 hour
  • The maximum acceptable data loss is 1 hour of transactions
  • Recovery must complete within 1 hour
  • The DR test runs every hour
Why: RPO defines the data-loss tolerance — the point in time to which we recover. RTO defines how fast we recover. A 1-hour RPO requires hourly (or more frequent) backups or replication. Stricter RPO = more expensive infrastructure.

Practice 2 — Site choice

A bank requiring sub-minute failover for its core trading system needs a:

  • Hot site
  • Warm site
  • Cold site
  • Mobile site
Why: Hot site is fully running with replicated data — failover is essentially instant. Warm site requires loading recent backups (hours). Cold site requires hardware installation (days/weeks). Cost matches RTO requirements.

Practice 3 — 3-2-1

The 3-2-1 backup rule requires:

  • 3 daily, 2 weekly, 1 monthly backup
  • 3 RAID disks, 2 controllers, 1 replication target
  • 3 copies on 2 different media with 1 stored offsite
  • 3-day RTO, 2-day RPO, 1 DR site
Why: Three copies (so a primary + two backups), two media types (e.g. disk + cloud, or disk + tape — protects against media-specific failure), one stored geographically separate (protects against site disaster, fire, flood). A simple, durable rule of thumb.

The "ARC" framework segregates which three duties?

  • Authorization, Recording, Custody
  • Approval, Reconciliation, Confirmation

Why: Authorization (approving), Recording (bookkeeping), Custody (handling the asset). Combining any two creates fraud opportunity. ARC.

Public companies must DISCLOSE in their 10-K under SOX 404:

  • All control deficiencies
  • Significant deficiencies
  • Inquiry findings

Question 5Audit testing

The strongest audit evidence about control operating effectiveness comes from:

  • Reperformance
  • Inquiry
  • Observation
  • Walkthroughs
Why: Reperformance — auditor independently re-runs the control. Inquiry is weakest; observation and walkthroughs are stronger but still limited to the moments observed.
Question 6SOX 302

SOX §302 requires:

  • Management's annual ICFR assertion
  • Quarterly CEO/CFO certification of FS and disclosure controls
  • External auditor attestation on ICFR
  • 7-year audit-records retention
Why: §302 = quarterly CEO/CFO certs. §404(a) = annual ICFR assertion. §404(b) = auditor attestation. §802 = records retention. Memorize the section numbers — they recur on the exam.
Question 7Audit committee

Under SOX, the audit committee must:

  • Be composed entirely of independent directors and engage the external auditor
  • Be chaired by the CEO
  • Include the CFO as a permanent member
  • Include at least one PCAOB member
Why: Audit committee must be 100% independent (no management). It engages and oversees the external auditor — moving that authority away from management was a core SOX reform.
Question 8ITGC

Which is an IT general control (NOT an application control)?

  • Edit check on a date field
  • Reconciliation of subledger to GL
  • Quarterly review of privileged user access
  • Hash total comparison
Why: Privileged-access review is an ITGC (access control). The other three are application/process-level controls. ITGCs cover the environment; application controls cover specific transactions.
Question 9Edit checks

A field that rejects ages above 120 is a:

  • Sequence check
  • Limit/reasonableness check
  • Validity check
  • Hash total
Why: Limit checks numeric bounds. Reasonableness compares against typical patterns. Validity tests whether the value is structurally valid (a real date, an existing customer ID).
Question 10Authentication

Combining a password with a fingerprint scan is an example of:

  • Multi-factor authentication (something you know + something you are)
  • Single-factor authentication
  • Federated identity
  • Single sign-on
Why: MFA combines factors from different categories: KNOW (password), HAVE (token/phone), ARE (biometric). Two passwords would still be one factor.
Question 11RTO/RPO

A 4-hour RTO means:

  • Backup runs every 4 hours
  • Maximum 4 hours of data loss is acceptable
  • Operations must resume within 4 hours of an outage
  • The DR test is performed every 4 hours
Why: RTO = recovery TIME objective = how fast operations resume. RPO = recovery POINT = how much data we can lose. Don't swap them.
Question 12DR site

A "warm site" differs from a "hot site" by:

  • Having hardware ready but not fully synced data
  • Being a fully replicated mirror with sub-minute failover
  • Being only a contracted office space
  • Being a mobile data center
Why: Warm = hardware ready, partial data, hours-to-recover. Hot = fully running, replicated, minutes. Cold = empty space, days/weeks.
